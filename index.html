Biscuit's Math Builder guide

Getting started

  1. Pick Adding + or Taking away −.
  2. Pick how hard: Warm-up (no trading), With trading, or Mix (like a quiz).
  3. Pick a way to solve: Base Ten Blocks, Expanded Form or Number Line, or Quiz Time for 10 problems in a row.
  4. Follow Biscuit one step at a time. A wrong answer gets a gentle hint, never a scolding.

The three ways

  • Base Ten Blocks: rods are tens and little cubes are ones. Trade 10 ones for a ten when adding; break a ten into 10 ones when taking away needs more ones.
  • Expanded Form: split each number into tens and ones (59 = 50 + 9), work on the tens, then the ones, then put them back together.
  • Number Line: start at the first number, then make big jumps of 10 and little hops of 1. Hop the ones first or jump the tens first.

"For grown-ups" on the home screen walks through each way with worked examples.

For grown-ups: a plain-English guide to each way

Every problem in the game is a 2-digit number plus or minus a 2-digit number, the kind second graders practice with base ten blocks, expanded form and a number line. The examples below are the ones Biscuit shows when you tap Watch Biscuit show me.

1. Base Ten Blocks

Think of it like play money. A rod is $10 and a small cube is $1. Ten $1 cubes can be traded for one $10 rod.

Adding: 52 + 19

  1. 52 is 5 rods and 2 cubes. 19 is 1 rod and 9 cubes.
  2. Put them together: 6 rods and 11 cubes.
  3. 11 cubes is too many for the ones place, so trade 10 cubes for 1 rod.
  4. Now there are 7 rods and 1 cube: 71.

Taking away: 52 − 19

  1. 52 is 5 rods and 2 cubes. We need to take away 1 rod and 9 cubes.
  2. You can't take 9 cubes from 2 cubes, so break 1 rod into 10 cubes.
  3. Now there are 4 rods and 12 cubes.
  4. Take away 9 cubes (3 left) and 1 rod (3 left): 33.

2. Expanded Form

Break each number into its tens and ones, work on each part, then put them back together.

Adding: 59 + 12

  1. Break them apart: 59 = 50 + 9 and 12 = 10 + 2.
  2. Add the tens: 50 + 10 = 60.
  3. Add the ones: 9 + 2 = 11.
  4. Put them together: 60 + 11 = 71.

Taking away: 52 − 19

  1. Break them apart: 52 = 50 + 2 and 19 = 10 + 9.
  2. There aren't enough ones to take away 9, so trade a ten: 50 + 2 becomes 40 + 12.
  3. Take away the tens: 40 − 10 = 30. Take away the ones: 12 − 9 = 3.
  4. Put them together: 30 + 3 = 33.

3. Number Line

Imagine hopping along a sidewalk with numbers painted on it. In the game, your child makes big jumps of 10 and little hops of 1.

Adding: 25 + 19 (19 is 1 ten and 9 ones)

  1. Start at 25.
  2. One way: hop +1 nine times (26, 27 … 34), then jump +10 to 44.
  3. Or jump +10 first to 35, then hop +1 nine times to 44. Both orders land in the same place.

Taking away: 44 − 19

  1. Start at 44 and go backward.
  2. Jump −10 to 34, then hop −1 nine times to 25.

4. Mix (like a quiz)

About half the problems need trading and half don't. Your child has to notice which kind each one is, the same as on a real test where nobody says whether trading is needed.

  • No trading: 34 + 15 = 49 and 76 − 12 = 64
  • Trading needed: 27 + 18 = 45 (7 + 8 makes more than 9 ones) and 52 − 19 = 33 (2 ones is not enough to take away 9)

Use Mix with Quiz Time to practice for a quiz or test.

5. Biscuit's voice and "Read it to me"

When Biscuit's voice (top of the screen) is On, Biscuit reads each problem, each step, each hint and his praise out loud as your child reaches it. When it is Off, your child can still tap a speaker button to hear the problem or the current step. This helps young readers and kids who learn better by hearing. Biscuit's voice is recorded and built into the game, so it works without the internet.

6. Biscuit's treats

Every finished problem earns a star. Every 5 stars, Biscuit gets a new treat that he wears or plays with: a bone, a red collar, a ball, a bandana, a party hat and sunglasses, then extra bones after that. The treat shelf on the home screen shows how many stars until the next one.
